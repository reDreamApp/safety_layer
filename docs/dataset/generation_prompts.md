# Critical Incident Generation Prompts

**Target:** 200 incidents (50 maladaptive per category × 2 types + 50 adaptive per category × 2 types)

**Difficulty Distribution per category:** 4 easy, 4 medium, 2 hard

---

## Maladaptive Categories

### 1. AVOIDANCE/FLIGHT (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer avoids unpleasant dream scenes without changing them (hiding, running away, closing eyes, freezing, doing nothing).

**Clinical Rationale:** Avoidance is a core PTSD Criterion C symptom (DSM-5, pp. 271-272) that prevents habituation and emotional processing.

**Expected Codes:** HIDE, ESCAPE

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clear, unambiguous avoidance behaviors:
- Single avoidance action
- No adaptive elements
- Examples: "I hide behind the tree", "I run away as fast as I can", "I close my eyes"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate cases requiring context interpretation:
- Multi-part responses with avoidance as primary
- Emotional acknowledgment + avoidance action
- Examples: "I'm terrified and I freeze, unable to move", "I keep running and hiding in different places"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Negation cases: "I decide NOT to hide this time" (tests negation handling - should be ADAPTIVE)
- Mixed responses: "I try to face it but then I hide" (tests triage)
- Ambiguous freezing: "I can't move" (freeze from fear vs. strategic stillness?)

---

### 2. INTERRUPTION/WAKING (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer terminates the dream experience by waking up, forcing themselves awake, or interrupting the nightmare.

**Clinical Rationale:** Dream interruption prevents completion of emotional processing and reinforces avoidance patterns.

**Expected Codes:** WAKE, INTERRUPT

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clear dream termination:
- Direct waking statements
- Examples: "I wake myself up", "I force myself awake", "I open my eyes and end the dream"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate cases with context complexity:
- Waking after failed adaptive attempt
- Examples: "When I can't escape, I wake myself up", "I realize it's a dream and decide to wake up"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Lucid awareness without waking: "I realize it's a dream but I stay in it to face it" (ADAPTIVE)
- Partial interruption: "I start to wake up but force myself to stay and deal with it" (ADAPTIVE)

---

### 3. VIOLENT REVENGE (10 incidents)

**Definition:** Excessive aggressive retaliation, gratuitous violence, or revenge-driven responses that go beyond proportionate self-defense.

**Clinical Rationale:** While assertive self-defense can be adaptive, excessive violence may indicate unprocessed anger and trauma.

**Expected Codes:** DESTROY, ATTACK

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clearly excessive violence:
- Gratuitous harm beyond threat level
- Revenge-driven motivation explicit
- Examples: "I keep hitting them even after they stop", "I destroy everything in rage", "I want them to suffer"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate cases with emotional context:
- Rage-driven responses
- Continued violence beyond neutralization
- Examples: "I attack them with everything I have and don't stop", "I fight back furiously until they're destroyed"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate proportionality boundary cases:
- Ambiguous self-defense: "I fight back hard to protect myself" (proportionate or excessive?)
- Escalation cases: "I push them away forcefully and they fall" (necessary force or excessive?)
- Intent ambiguity: "I make sure they can never hurt anyone again" (safety or revenge?)

---

### 4. SUPPRESSION (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer denies emotional impact of clearly distressing content, claims neutrality toward threatening situations, or suppresses natural fear responses.

**Clinical Rationale:** Emotional suppression prevents genuine emotional processing and maintains PTSD symptomatology.

**Expected Codes:** SUPPRESS, DENY

**CRITICAL:** Suppression detection requires nightmare context showing distressing content + user claiming neutrality.

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clear emotional denial:
- Obvious mismatch between context severity and claimed emotional state
- Examples:
  - Context: "At my pet's funeral" / Input: "I'm completely fine with it"
  - Context: "Being attacked" / Input: "It doesn't bother me at all"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate subtler denial:
- Minimization of threat
- Intellectualization
- Examples: "It's not a big deal", "I tell myself it doesn't matter", "I push the feelings away"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Genuine emotional regulation vs suppression: "I acknowledge I'm scared but choose to stay calm" (ADAPTIVE - emotional mastery)
- Healthy acceptance vs denial: "I've made peace with this loss" (context-dependent)

---

### 5. TRAUMA REPLAY (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer rehearses the nightmare exactly as it occurred, replays traumatic memories without change, or selects trauma nightmares for IRT despite contraindication.

**Clinical Rationale:** Per Krakow IRT protocol, patients should NOT rescript nightmares that directly replay trauma as this may worsen symptoms.

**Expected Codes:** REPLAY, FREEZE

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate explicit trauma replay:
- Direct statements of reliving trauma
- No changes to traumatic content
- Examples: "The accident happens exactly as it did before", "It's the same nightmare I've had since the trauma", "Nothing changes, it replays identically"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate replay with awareness:
- Recognizes it's a replay but doesn't change it
- Examples: "I know this is what happened and I see it again unchanged", "The traumatic event unfolds the same way every time"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Replay with minor detail changes: "It's mostly the same but the car is a different color" (still replay)
- Replay awareness with attempt to change: "It starts as the same trauma but I try to change the ending" (ADAPTIVE attempt)

---

## Adaptive Categories

### 6. CONFRONTATIONAL MASTERY (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer performs an action to alter the dream course to their advantage: fighting back proportionately, winning over threat, making behavioral attempts to obtain assistance.

**Clinical Rationale:** Active engagement with the threat promotes mastery and reduces conditioned fear response.

**Expected Codes:** CONFRONT, CHALLENGE

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clear confrontational action:
- Direct facing of threat
- Proportionate self-defense
- Examples: "I turn and face them", "I stand my ground", "I fight back to defend myself"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate cases with emotional complexity:
- Fear acknowledged + confrontational action
- Examples: "I'm scared but I stand firm", "Despite my fear, I face the threat"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Confrontation vs avoidance: "I look directly at it to understand what it wants" (confrontational or just looking?)
- Verbal vs physical: "I tell them they can't hurt me" (assertion or suppression?)

---

### 7. HELP-SEEKING (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer calls for help, seeks social support, or enlists others to address the threat.

**Clinical Rationale:** Social support-seeking is an adaptive coping strategy that reduces isolation.

**Expected Codes:** HELP, SUPPORT

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clear help-seeking:
- Direct calls for assistance
- Examples: "I call for help", "I ask someone to help me", "I reach out to others for support"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate cases with complexity:
- Help-seeking after initial fear
- Multiple help-seeking attempts
- Examples: "I'm scared so I call out for help", "I try to find someone who can help me"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Help-seeking + escape: "I call for help but when no one comes I wake up" (tests triage - should be UNSAFE due to interruption)
- Appropriate vs dependent: "I try myself first, then ask for help" (adaptive progression)

---

### 8. ENVIRONMENTAL MASTERY (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer changes the dream environment to their advantage: creating safe spaces, transforming threatening locations, or manipulating physical surroundings.

**Clinical Rationale:** Environmental control demonstrates agency and creative problem-solving.

**Expected Codes:** CHANGE, TRANSFORM

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clear environmental transformation:
- Direct environment modification
- Examples: "I change the dark forest into a bright meadow", "I create a protective barrier", "I transform the scary place into somewhere safe"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate cases with complexity:
- Multi-step environmental changes
- Examples: "I make the room lighter and add a door to escape through", "I change the threatening environment piece by piece"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Environmental change vs escape: "I create an exit and leave" (mastery or avoidance?)
- Symbolic vs concrete changes: "I make the darkness fade away" (environmental or emotional?)

---

### 9. EMOTIONAL MASTERY (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer regulates their emotional response to the threat: calming themselves, managing fear, or choosing courage despite anxiety.

**Clinical Rationale:** Emotional regulation is a core skill for trauma recovery.

**Expected Codes:** RELAX, CALM

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clear emotional regulation:
- Direct emotional self-regulation
- Examples: "I take a deep breath and calm myself", "I tell myself to relax", "I manage my fear and stay calm"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate cases with complexity:
- Emotional regulation in response to threat
- Examples: "I'm scared but I breathe deeply and feel calmer", "I acknowledge my fear and choose to be calm"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Emotional mastery vs suppression: "I feel the fear but don't let it control me" (regulation or denial?)
- Authentic vs forced: "I'm completely calm despite the threat" (genuine regulation or suppression?)

---

### 10. MYTHICAL MASTERY (10 incidents)

**Definition:** The dreamer invokes supernatural powers, divine intervention, or magical abilities to resolve the threat.

**Clinical Rationale:** Symbolic empowerment through mythical elements can facilitate psychological mastery.

**Expected Codes:** POWER, DIVINE

**NOTE:** Does NOT include lucid dreaming (removed from ontology).

#### Easy (4 incidents)
Generate clear supernatural intervention:
- Explicit magical powers
- Divine/spiritual protection
- Examples: "I summon magical powers to protect me", "An angel appears and helps me", "I gain superpowers and stop the threat"

#### Medium (4 incidents)
Generate cases with complexity:
- Invoking powers + using them
- Examples: "I call on divine protection and feel safe", "I imagine I have powers and use them to change things"

#### Hard (2 incidents)
Generate boundary cases:
- Mythical vs environmental: "I make the threat disappear with magic" (supernatural power or dream control?)
- Realistic vs mythical: "I find unexpected strength within me" (inner strength or supernatural?)

## Quality Control Criteria

For each incident, verify:

### Clinical Accuracy
- ✓ User input matches ontology definition
- ✓ Difficulty calibration is appropriate
- ✓ No category confusion

### Realism
- ✓ Nightmare context is clinically plausible
- ✓ User input sounds authentic
- ✓ Age-appropriate content
- ✓ Pronouns resolvable from context

### Testing Value
- ✓ Tests specific classifier challenge
- ✓ Not redundant
- ✓ Provides clear evaluation signal

### Format
- ✓ JSON structure correct
- ✓ All fields present
- ✓ ID follows convention

## Generation Workflow

**Category-by-Category Approach:**

For each category:
1. Review definition and rationale
2. Generate 4 easy incidents
3. Generate 4 medium incidents
4. Generate 2 hard incidents
5. Quality control review
6. Refine as needed

**Order:**
1. Avoidance/Flight
2. Interruption/Waking
3. Violent Revenge
4. Suppression
5. Trauma Replay
6. Confrontational Mastery
7. Help-Seeking
8. Environmental Mastery
9. Emotional Mastery
