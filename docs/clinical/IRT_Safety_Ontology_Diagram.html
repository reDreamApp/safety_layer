Dream Response Classification

Binary Safety Decision: SAFE vs. UNSAFE

✓

Adaptive Mastery (SAFE)

From Multidimensional Mastery Scale (Germain et al., 2004)

Behavioral Mastery

SAFE

Dreamer performs an action to alter the course of the dream to their advantage: fighting back, winning over the threat/aggressor, or making behavioral attempts to obtain assistance.

Examples: "I decided to stand my ground and face the figure" / "I fought back and pushed the attacker away"

MMS: Behavioral Mastery subscale

Social Mastery

SAFE

Dreamer changes personality aspects of dream characters, removes threatening characters, changes nature of interaction, adds helpful new character or nonthreatening social setting, or is assisted by another character on request.

Examples: "I called for my friend who came to help" / "The threatening figure transformed into someone kind"

MMS: Social Mastery subscale

Environmental Mastery

SAFE

Dreamer changes the physical environment to a nonthreatening setting, makes the initial dream environment impermeable to threat, or a spontaneous helpful event occurs (without being requested).

Examples: "The dark room transformed into a sunny garden" / "I created a protective barrier around myself"

MMS: Environmental Mastery subscale

Emotional Mastery

SAFE

Dreamer changes the overall dream affect or their emotional reactions to specific dream characters, events, or settings.

Examples: "I felt my fear dissolve into calmness" / "Instead of panic, I chose to feel curious"

MMS: Emotional Mastery subscale

Mythical/Cognitive Mastery

SAFE

Supernatural figure/event intervenes in favor of dreamer (releases from threat, terminates threat, reassures), OR dreamer uses supernatural powers to overcome threat, OR gains lucidity/insight (realizes it's a dream).

Examples: "A guardian angel appeared and protected me" / "I realized this was a dream and I had control"

MMS: Mythical Mastery subscale + Lucid Dreaming literature

✗

Maladaptive Patterns (UNSAFE)

From IRT literature (Krakow) & MMS Avoidance subscale

Avoidance / Flight

UNSAFE

Dreamer avoids unpleasant dream scenes within the dream without changing them (e.g., closing eyes, running away, hiding). Does not resolve threat - reinforces fear response.

Examples: "I closed my eyes so I wouldn't see it" / "I kept running as fast as I could" / "I hid under the bed"

MMS: Avoidance subscale; PTSD avoidance criterion

Interruption / Waking

UNSAFE

Dreamer awakens from the dream to escape perceived threats. Prevents processing and maintains fear association with nightmare content.

Examples: "I forced myself to wake up" / "I died to stop the horror" / "The dream just ended before resolution"

MMS: Avoidance subscale (awakening variant); Krakow IRT protocol

Violent Revenge

UNSAFE

Excessive aggressive retaliation, gratuitous violence, or revenge-driven responses that go beyond self-defense. Differs from Behavioral Mastery by degree and motivation.

Examples: "I brutally destroyed the attacker out of pure rage" / "I tortured them for what they did"

IRT clinical guidance (Krakow); H/V Aggression subscale

Suppression

UNSAFE

Attempting to block, forget, or push away nightmare content rather than process it. Mental avoidance that maintains the nightmare pattern.

Examples: "I tried not to think about it" / "I blocked the memory from my mind"

PTSD avoidance criterion; IRT clinical guidance

Trauma Replay

UNSAFE

Rescripted dream that replays traumatic event without meaningful change. IRT specifically instructs patients NOT to select nightmares that replay trauma.

Examples: "The assault happened again exactly as before" / "I relived the accident with no change"

Krakow IRT protocol: explicit contraindication

Classification Logic

SAFE: All identified actions map to Adaptive Mastery patterns
UNSAFE: Any action maps to a Maladaptive pattern (conservative approach per IRT principles)

Suggested Alternative Strategies

Important Caveat: These suggested pairings are clinically-informed hypotheses, NOT evidence-based prescriptions. IRT is deliberately non-directive—patients are instructed to "change the nightmare any way you wish." These suggestions are intended as potential therapeutic guidance if a patient is struggling to generate alternatives, and require clinical validation before implementation.

Maladaptive Pattern Suggested Alternative Clinical Rationale
Avoidance / Flight Environmental or Behavioral Mastery Instead of fleeing, transform the threatening environment into a safe space, or take assertive action to neutralize the threat.
Violent Revenge Social Mastery Shift focus from destroying the aggressor to interpersonal resolution—transform the threatening character, bring in allies, or de-escalate through dialogue.
Interruption / Waking Mythical/Cognitive Mastery Instead of escaping by waking, gain lucid awareness that it's a dream, invoke protective imagery, or use dream-logic to resolve the scenario.
Suppression Emotional Mastery Instead of blocking emotions, actively transform the emotional tone—allow fear to shift to calm, or anxiety to curiosity.
Trauma Replay Environmental Mastery Fundamentally alter the setting or circumstances so the traumatic scenario cannot unfold as before. Note: Direct trauma processing should be done with clinical supervision.

These suggestions are derived from clinical reasoning based on MMS dimensions and IRT principles, pending empirical validation.

Questions for Clinical Validation

  1. Category Completeness: Are there any adaptive mastery patterns or maladaptive response patterns that are missing from this ontology?
  2. Boundary Cases: Is the distinction between "Behavioral Mastery" (adaptive) and "Violent Revenge" (maladaptive) clinically meaningful? How should we operationalize this boundary?
  3. Emotional Mastery: Should explicit statements of negative emotions (without resolution) be classified as maladaptive, or are they neutral?
  4. Mixed Responses: How should we handle dream rescripts that contain BOTH adaptive and maladaptive elements? (Current approach: flag as UNSAFE if ANY maladaptive element is present)
  5. Severity Gradation: Should certain maladaptive patterns trigger stronger warnings than others? (e.g., Trauma Replay vs. simple Avoidance)
  6. Alternative Strategies: Are the suggested alternative strategy pairings clinically appropriate? Would you recommend different adaptive strategies for any of the maladaptive patterns?
